I have built four workbenches and been wrong about each of them in a different way, so before I build the fifth I am writing down what I think I know.

## What the last four taught me

1. **The first was too light.** It walked across the floor when I planed on it. A bench is the one tool in the shop whose job is to not move.
2. **The second was too clever.** It had a tool well, two vises, a sliding deadman and a row of drawers. I used the flat part.
3. **The third was too tall.** I built it at the height that felt right standing next to it, which is not the height that is right when you are leaning your weight onto a plane.
4. **The fourth is fine,** and I have stopped noticing it, which may be the highest thing you can say about a bench.

## Open questions

- Is a thick top actually better, or only heavier? Aldous says heavier is the point.
- One vise or none. I have been using holdfasts for a year and have not missed the vise once. But a year is not long.
- How much of what I want is a better bench and how much is wanting to build a bench?

That last one I should think about before I buy any more maple.
